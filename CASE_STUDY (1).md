# Case Study Notes --- From Classifieds to Transactions

## Purpose

This document provides additional context for readers who want to
understand the managerial logic behind the portfolio workbook.

## The transformation thesis

The starting point is an established automotive classifieds business
whose core economic role is generating visibility and leads.

The strategic opportunity is to move further down the transaction funnel
without adopting an inventory-heavy dealer model.

The proposed architecture is therefore **asset-light but
transaction-heavy**:

-   retain the marketplace's audience and inventory advantages
-   introduce trust and transaction services
-   use specialist partners for regulated or risk-bearing capabilities
-   monetize several layers of the transaction
-   preserve capital efficiency by avoiding vehicle ownership

## Why the incumbent starting point matters

A greenfield two-sided marketplace must solve both supply and demand
simultaneously.

An established classifieds platform may already possess:

-   organic search visibility
-   direct traffic
-   brand recognition
-   listing inventory
-   buyer intent
-   seller intent
-   historical behavioral data

The strategic question is whether those inherited assets can lower the
cost and risk of launching a transactional proposition.

## Build vs. partner logic

The model distinguishes capabilities that can create strategic
differentiation from capabilities that are better provided by regulated
or specialist partners.

### Marketplace should own

-   customer journey
-   transaction orchestration
-   product UX
-   pricing / packaging
-   data layer
-   customer relationship
-   partner integration
-   conversion optimization

### Potential partner capabilities

-   escrow / safeguarded payments
-   identity / KYC
-   official ownership transfer
-   mechanical inspection
-   warranty underwriting
-   vehicle-history data
-   consumer finance
-   vehicle logistics

This keeps the platform close to the customer while avoiding unnecessary
regulatory, actuarial and operational risk.

## Financial logic

The model does not rely on one revenue stream.

Instead it asks how much contribution can be created around a completed
transaction and how adoption of ancillary services changes ARPU.

This is then tested against:

-   transaction acquisition cost
-   contribution margin
-   operating expenses
-   marketing ramp
-   cash requirements
-   break-even volume
-   LTV/CAC

## Why bilateral CAC matters

Marketplace economics can be misleading if customer acquisition is
treated as one number.

A C2C transaction needs both a vehicle/seller and a buyer.

The model therefore separates acquisition effort by side and asks
whether inherited SEO and marketplace liquidity can create a structural
advantage relative to an inventory-owning operator.

## Scenario logic

Rather than treating the base case as a forecast, the model includes
downside and upside scenarios and a two-variable sensitivity matrix.

The point is to expose the strategic dependency:

**If market capture or monetization falls short, how quickly does the
investment case deteriorate?**

This turns the model into a tool for management discussion.

## Validation priorities before investment

A real implementation should validate at least:

1.  Current C2C market size and eligible online segment.
2.  Seller willingness to use an integrated transaction journey.
3.  Buyer willingness to pay / adopt trust services.
4.  Lead-to-transaction conversion.
5.  Seller and buyer CAC separately.
6.  Real partner economics for escrow, transfer, inspection, warranty
    and finance.
7.  Regulatory structure and liability allocation.
8.  ARPU achievable without damaging marketplace liquidity.
9.  Operational complexity at increasing transaction volume.
10. The amount of launch marketing genuinely required given inherited
    organic traffic.

## AI-assisted workflow

AI was valuable for accelerating research, structuring alternatives,
testing assumptions and helping construct an integrated model.

The most important work remained managerial: choosing the questions,
deciding which evidence mattered, identifying weak assumptions and
translating analysis into an invest / test / redesign decision.
